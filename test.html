Skills Employers Seek

Job seekers need a range of skills and the ability to apply these skills and knowledge in the workplace. Many employers are concerned that far too many job seekers are inadequately prepared to be successful in the workplace. The most sought after skills are listed below.

critical thinking. Exercise sound reasoning and analytical thinking; use knowledge, facts, and data to solve workplace problems. verbal communications. Articulate thoughts, ideas clearly and effectively; have public speaking skills. written communications. Read and write memos, letters, and other documents clearly and effectively. teamwork & collaboration. Build collaborative relationships with colleagues; capable of working with diverse teams; manage conflicts. diversity. Introduction to Soft Skills

Soft skills are personal characteristics that allow us to relate positively to others. These skills enhance our personal interactions and lead to greater job performance and overall satisfaction. Unlike hard skills, soft skills are easily transferable to any job, organization, or industry. Therefore, they are an investment worth making. Oftentimes, we place the focus of our career development efforts on hard skills and other skills that specifically relate to our ability to get work-related tasks done. As a result, we may neglect to develop our soft skills.

Some examples of soft skills include listening skills, mindfulness, personableness, professional networking abilities, and customer service skills. Applying soft skills can help you to build stronger work relationships, work more productively, and maximize your career prospects. SOFT SKILLS ARE LEARNED While some soft skills come more easily to some than they might to others, they are not innate. Like all skills, they must be learned. Although some soft skills may be more difficult to learn than others, we develop soft skills in the same way we develop any other skill—through practice. Spending time with those who seem to effortlessly demonstrate a soft skill that you find challenging to grasp is one way to build your soft skill set. PROFESSIONALISM The term professionalism may conjure up images of a cold and harsh person, but professionalism is a key soft skill. It doesn’t require you to be inauthentic or detached. A person demonstrating professionalism conducts himself with responsibility, integrity, accountability, and excellence. He discovers ways to be productive and communicate effectively; he reports to work on time, returns promptly from breaks, and dresses appropriately. He is clean, neat, and organized. He speaks clearly and politely to everyone with whom he interacts. A person displaying professionalism knows that in order to achieve the goals within a given task, he must use clearly defined methods. This includes setting objectives, mindful decision-making, planning, and scheduling. Once the tasks are completed, he evaluates the success of his methods. The mastering the skill of professionalism most sought after skills are listed in the table below. be consistent. An important characteristic of the workplace professional is his ability to be consistent. Changing your values and behavior can cause you to become confused about what you truly value, and others will question your authenticity. complete work to a deadline. Make sure tasks are completed in a timely manner. It is easy to feel out of control when you disregard the plan you should follow and the deadline you need to work toward. be flexible. You’re sticking to the plan and working toward your deadline when you realize things are not going according to plan. This is your chance to display your flexibility so that you can get things back on track. self education. Self-education is the process of acquiring knowledge and skills independently, without formal instruction, often through reading, informal study, and self-directed learning.  stay fit. The ability to stay fit is an important soft skill. Exercising your body and mind is crucial to professionalism. Failure to do so can lead to emotional, mental, and physical illnesses.

Mindful Decision Making

Mindful decision-making combines reason with intuition to come up with choices that are practical. Choices need to be made carefully and mindfully. In stressful situations, it is easy to make decisions based on emotions, stress, external pressure, and finances. Therefore, it’s imperative to recognize events that increase the risk of making a poor decision that can have both long term and negative consequences. MINDFUL DECISION-MAKING TIPS Identify the type of decision you’re making and then begin gathering information Once you have all the information, listen to your intuition, then make your decision. Throughout this process it’s imperative to pay close attention to your feelings. This allows you to understand any feelings of conflict, as well as their source. The source of the conflict may be revealed as you practice being mindful. Recognizing the conflict will help you make the decision without fear. Reflect on previous decisions. Think of some decisions you’ve made that were based on your emotions, stress, external pressure, or finances. Now, consider how mindful decision-making could have altered your decisions. THE HABITS OF CONSIDERATE PEOPLE they practice empathy Considerate people are capable of putting themselves in other people’s shoes. Their sense of compassion for others drives them to connect. They derive personal joy and satisfaction from this selfless exchange. they smile often Smiling can have a significant impact on how others perceive you. Smile for the positive impact it can have on others. they Are Intuitive As you channel your sense of empathy and consider how others around you are feeling, choose to act on that information. Simply asking someone how they're doing can do wonders for their mood and self-esteem. they are polite Being polite doesn't begin and end with saying please, thank you, and you're welcome. It involves an acknowledgment of another person's feelings and then behaving accordingly. Follow the golden rule and treat others like you want to be treated—from being punctual or respecting others' time; to not talking over others or exercising self-control; to actively listening to what others have to say. they put others first Selflessness can be a double-edged sword for considerate people. While prioritizing the needs of others over your own makes people happy and creates a sense of fulfillment, you can lose the ability to take care of yourself. Striking that balance is just as important as being considerate. Otherwise, you fall into the realm of people-pleasing, which leads to a decrease in your own productivity and self-esteem. they compromise The ability to cooperate is a skill that will present plenty of opportunities for you to master, because it is an unavoidable aspect of dealing with others in business and personal relationships. Compromise can be challenging when you are in a position of less influence. You may be expected to follow the lead of your superiors, without regard for your own feelings and opinions. Nevertheless, learning to hold on to your beliefs and high standards, while accepting the ideas of others, is crucial to your success in the workplace. they apologize Some people apologize for fear of offending others. Others forgo apologies altogether, coming across as rude and insensitive. Similar to the people-pleasing tendencies of kind and considerate people, apologies must be balanced. Being considerate means apologizing when you realize you’ve made a mistake. But when you’re a people pleaser, or obsequious, the only person you’re harming is yourself.
